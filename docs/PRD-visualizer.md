# PRD: Visualizer (gh issue #3)

## Goal

Give the player a visualizer. It sits in the lower band, under the transport, in a tab that switches it with the playlist. It shows the track name, offers a few visual modes with sliders to tune them, and can go fullscreen while the artist and title stay on screen.

## Requirements

- **Placement:** a `Playlist | Visualizer` tab row sits above the lower band. Only one is shown at a time. The Visualizer tab replaces the playlist and never changes the playlist's own behaviour.
- **Modes:** `Spectrum` (log-spaced bars, theme accent gradient), `Wave` (oscilloscope trace), `Fractal` (an audio-reactive Julia set, rendered at low resolution and stretched by the GPU), `Stereo` (left and right bars back to back, the left channel on the left, bass at the centre and treble outward), and `Scope` (a goniometer that plots side against mid: mono is a vertical line and a wide mix fills out). `Wave` draws the right channel in the second accent colour under the left in the first, so a stereo mix shows that colour wherever the sides differ. `Spectrum` bars split into a left and right half, so a stereo mix shows its imbalance; mono bars are whole. The fractal represents the music and has no clock of its own. Silence is solid black with nothing moving; a held sound holds its shape. The Julia constant is a pure function of bass, mid and treble, plus two pieces of event memory: each bass onset flings it by a short random wander that springs back, and a section change (the spectrum drifting from its slow average) glides its centre to a curated anchor and picks a new wedge count and Phoenix offset. Both come from the music, not a clock; silence and reduce-motion hold them still. The kaleidoscope (4 to 8 mirrored wedges, six by default) rotates only on spectral flux, so a steady tone stops it and each new note turns it. Its rings glow with the spectrum: bass at the centre, treble at the rim. The background shape is the spectrum wrapped around it: each angle is a frequency band, with bass at the bottom and treble at the top, and the left half follows the left channel and the right half the right, so a lopsided mix bulges to one side, so a bass note bulges the bottom and bright treble the top. Its depth follows the gated level and kick punches, and it eases so it morphs; in silence and under reduce-motion it is the plain circle. The fractal reads the bars through a soft compression (`v^0.7`), so quiet passages still show; Spectrum and Wave use the raw bars. Ripple follows the treble, zoom and brightness follow the overall level, and bass onsets punch briefly. Clicks and ticks, too brief for the bars, are caught by a separate detector on the raw samples: they flash the fractal, fling its constant, and lift every Spectrum bar to a low floor, all off under reduce motion. Hue follows the spectral centroid (dark to bright sound) on top of the Hue slider. A Phoenix creature appears only while the mids are present, and is pulled from its classic shape by the mids and treble. Trails fade faster as the music drops, so silence clears the screen. Actors are a cast of up to 24 small Julia blobs, each a soft disc with its own anchor constant and hue. Pitch shapes them: a higher band's actors are smaller, brighter, shorter-lived and shimmer (flicker), and a higher band's onset brings more of them and sooner again, so treble becomes a bright, shimmering swarm and bass a few large, dim blobs. A new sound brings one on: a bass onset brings an extra, a higher band's onset brings sparks, and a click brings a spark; a section change retires the cast and brings a new lead. Actors age with the music (flux and kicks push them along, a held tone holds them still), release when the band that drew them goes quiet, and fade out. Silence clears them, and under reduce-motion none spawn. Placement follows the stereo field: each new actor appears on the side its sound came from at the onset (left speaker, left of the stage; right, right; centred sound in the middle), with a little spread so a burst fans out. Section leads sit by the whole mix's balance, clicks by the top band's. An actor keeps drifting toward its side while its sound lasts, and a wide mix fans a burst out where a mono mix keeps it tight. A sound that lives on one side counts at full strength for onsets, sections, flux and clicks, so a hard-panned hit still lights the picture. A click flashes the side it came from, each half of the fractal ripples on its own treble, and a right-heavy mix brightens the right edge a little and shades the colour warm on the left and cool on the right in a wide mix. A kick leans the constant toward the bass's side, the Phoenix leans toward the mids' side, the kaleidoscope turns a touch faster toward a right-heavy mix, and actors take a slight warm or cool hue from their side. All of these are small nudges and vanish for mono content. A lone tone, such as a solo sine or a single instrument's line, also lights the picture: the silence gate, onsets, flux and section changes take each band's peak as well as its mean, so a sparse sound isn't drowned by the empty bars around it. Dense music keeps its old values, and a held tone still holds everything still.
- **Track name:** the visual carries the current track name in a strip at its top-left. With nothing loaded it reads "Nothing playing". Radio shows the station and the ICY song title.
- **Controls:** three mode buttons, a `Fullscreen` button, and four sliders: `Gain` (25-400%), `Smooth` (0-100, how slowly bars fall), `Bars` (8-96), and `Hue` (0-100, rotates the gradient or the fractal palette).
- **Fullscreen:** `F11` or the button toggles it, using the desktop fullscreen mode. The visual fills the screen and keeps the track name on it. The controls float along the bottom while the pointer moves, and hide after about 3 s of stillness. `Esc` leaves fullscreen first, then behaves as before.
- **Persistence:** the mode, the four sliders and the active tab are saved to `settings.ini`. Changes are written after the control settles (about 0.5 s), and on quit. A missing or unreadable value falls back to its default.
- **Reduced motion:** under `MEDIAPLAYER_REDUCE_MOTION` the fractal still follows the bands for shape and brightness, but the kick, flux rotation and ripple are off, so it never moves on its own.
- **Idle cost:** nothing is sampled or drawn while the Visualizer tab is hidden and fullscreen is off.
- **Constraints:** C99, no new dependencies. The audio callback takes no locks and allocates nothing: the visualizer's samples travel through rings of `SDL_atomic_t` int16 values: a mono tap, plus left and right taps for stereo placement.

## Out of scope

- Plugin or user-supplied visual modes.
- Per-mode colour pickers. Colour comes from the theme accent plus the Hue slider.
- Splitting the title into separate artist and title fields. The visual shows the same text as the status row.

## Tasks

1. Audio: a mono sample tap plus left and right taps in `audio_callback` (`src/playback.c`), read through `playback_get_samples` and `playback_get_samples_lr`.
2. Maths: `src/viz.c` (FFT, log bars, smoothing, hue, fractal constant), with `tests/test_viz.c`.
3. Settings: a generic int key/value store in `src/settings.c` that keeps unknown keys, with `tests/test_settings.c` covering it.
4. Drawing: `src/visualizer.c`, using Nuklear canvas and image commands and one streamed texture for the fractal.
5. UI: tab row, fullscreen view and controls, F11 and Esc handling in `src/main.c`.
6. Docs: this PRD, the non-goal lines in `docs/PRD.md` and `docs/PRD-visual-polish.md`, README controls and module list, CONTRIBUTING architecture table.
